About Kaal Sarp Dosh Puja (काल सर्प दोष)

This puja is performed to reduce delays, obstacles and anxiety attributed to Rahu–Ketu positions in a kundli. Remedy ritual for Kaal Sarp Dosh in the birth chart. Families choose this puja at important moments of life or when starting something new, and it is usually performed with the whole household present so that everyone takes part in the sankalp, aarti and prasad.

The details of the ritual can differ between regions, languages and family traditions, which is why we ask about your sampradaya at the time of booking. The pandit then follows the order of steps your family is used to rather than a single fixed script.

When is Kaal Sarp Dosh Puja usually performed?

There is no single date that suits every family. A pandit usually looks at the tithi, nakshatra, weekday and the family’s Panchang tradition, and also considers practical points such as the guests’ availability and the venue. For kaal sarp dosh puja, we recommend asking for muhurat options at least a week ahead, and earlier in wedding season or festival weeks, when pandit availability is tight.

If you do not have a date in mind, choose “help me find a date” in the form and give us a range of days. We will suggest the best muhurats for your city.

How the puja usually flows

The exact order depends on tradition, but kaal sarp dosh puja typically takes about 3–4 hours and follows a pattern like this:

  1. Sankalp: the family states its names, gotra, place and purpose of the puja.
  2. Ganesh puja and kalash sthapana: invoking Lord Ganesh and the sacred water pot to remove obstacles.
  3. Main puja: worship of the central deity or ritual of the day with the correct mantras.
  4. Havan or offering (where applicable): offerings into the sacred fire with chanting.
  5. Aarti and pushpanjali: the family offers flowers and light together.
  6. Prasad and dakshina: prasad is shared and the pandit is honoured with dakshina.

Samagri: what you need

The usual samagri for kaal sarp dosh puja includes silver or copper naag-naagin, black sesame, milk, flowers, kalash, coconut, rice, havan samagri, blue/black cloth. Fresh flowers, fruits and leaves are best bought on the morning of the puja. If you prefer not to arrange these yourself, choose the “with samagri” option and the pandit will bring a complete kit; the page shows both prices. If you are collecting items yourself, ask the pandit for a final list a day ahead so nothing is missed.

How to prepare your home for Kaal Sarp Dosh Puja

  • Clean the space and keep a clean, level area ready, ideally facing east or north as your pandit advises.
  • Keep a low seat or mat for the pandit and enough seating for the family.
  • Keep names and gotra of family members ready for the sankalp.
  • For apartments, check if a havan or open flame is permitted and inform neighbours.
  • Keep drinking water, a place for the pandit to rest and containers for prasad.
  • Switch phones to silent and ask guests to arrive a little before the muhurat.

Choosing a pandit: what to ask

A good pandit explains what is being done and why, arrives on time, and respects the family’s tradition. When you book kaal sarp dosh puja through us you can ask about the language, how many years of experience the pandit has, whether the pandit brings the samagri and whether the pandit can explain the mantras in simple words. We confirm all this on WhatsApp before you pay. Our pandits do not pressure families into extra rituals; if something additional is genuinely recommended, it is explained first.

Can Kaal Sarp Dosh Puja be done online?

Yes, if you live abroad or the pandit cannot travel, a pandit can lead the puja by video, perform the sankalp in your name and gotra and guide you through each step. For many families, an online puja followed by sharing prasad with relatives works well. Ask us for the online option when booking.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Booking too late for popular dates and festival weeks.
  • Fixing guests and venue before confirming the muhurat.
  • Not asking the pandit for the samagri list in advance.
  • Forgetting to tell the pandit the family tradition or language.
  • Assuming one ritual order fits every region.
