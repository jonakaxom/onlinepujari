What “privacy” means for a puja booking

A puja booking involves personal details: where you live, your family’s names and sometimes your gotra and birth-related information. We treat these as private. We collect only what is needed to arrange your booking, we do not sell it, and we keep the process transparent. When a pandit is assigned to your booking, only the details needed for that puja are shared with that pandit, such as your name, phone, address and date.

If you sign in with Google, Google handles your password; we never see it. We rely on Google sign-in and a secure database to store your account details, and we limit who can read them.

Security and children

Access to our admin tools is limited to our team. Pandits see only the bookings assigned to them. Customers see only their own bookings. If you suspect misuse of your details, tell us immediately and we will look into it. Our service is meant for adults arranging pujas for their families; if a child’s details are needed, such as for a mundan or annaprasan, they should be given by a parent or guardian.

Who we are and what this page is for

Online Pujari helps families across India and abroad find a verified pandit for pujas and sanskar, at home, in a society hall, at an office or shop, or online. This plain-language guide sits beside our formal policy to help you understand it quickly. It is an explanation, not a legal document: if any detail here ever differs from the policy text above, the policy text above applies.

We keep our language simple on purpose. Booking a puja is often an emotional and time-sensitive decision for a family, and you should never need a lawyer to understand what you are agreeing to.

How a booking works

You choose a puja, a city, a date range and the language you prefer, using the booking form or WhatsApp. We then match a pandit, check the muhurat for your city and send you the plan on WhatsApp. You pay only after the pandit and muhurat are confirmed. Prices shown on the website are starting prices, and the final amount can vary with the city, the samagri you choose and the number of pandits needed. We confirm the total before you pay.

The pandits who perform your puja are independent professionals whom we connect you with. We take care to speak to every pandit before listing them and to confirm the plan with you, but the ritual itself is carried out by the pandit according to your family’s tradition.

The details we ask for, and why

To arrange a puja we ask for your name, phone number, city, preferred date and puja type, plus your language and, if you wish, your gotra for the sankalp. We use these details only to arrange your booking and to contact you about it. We do not sell your data, and we do not ask for government ID numbers, bank passwords or card numbers through the website.

If you create an account, you sign in with Google. We store the name, email and phone number connected to your account so that you can see your bookings, and pandits can see the details of bookings assigned to them so they can reach you on time.

Your choices and how to reach us

You can ask us to correct or delete the details we hold about you by messaging us on WhatsApp or through the contact page. You can also stop using the service at any time. If something about a booking, a payment or a pandit does not feel right, tell us straight away; we would rather fix it early than have you wait.

The website remembers one small preference in your own browser, your light or dark mode choice. It stays on your device and is not sent to us.

Respect for every tradition

Hindu practice differs between regions, languages and family lineages. A Bengali Lakshmi puja, a Tamil Ganapathi homam and a Gujarati vastu pujan share a spirit but not a script. We do not push a single template. Please tell us your family’s sampradaya and customs when you book so that the pandit prepares the correct sankalp and sequence, and let us know if anything needs to be adapted for the space you have.

Dates, muhurat and festivals

The auspicious time for a puja is chosen from the tithi, nakshatra, weekday and the almanac your family follows. Because local sunrise changes from one city to another, we confirm the muhurat for your own city. During festival weeks and wedding season pandits book out quickly, so we suggest asking at least a week ahead, and earlier for Diwali, Ganesh Chaturthi and Navratri. Our festival calendar lists the main dates, which follow the New Delhi panchang.
